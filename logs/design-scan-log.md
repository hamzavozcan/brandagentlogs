# Design Scan Log

## Scan 1 — 2026-09-14

### Trends

1. **Controlled distressing over heavy distressing.** The market has shifted away from allover destruction toward selective, intentional distressing — a single frayed hem, cracked print lettering, or one ripped panel set against otherwise clean fabric. Buyers read controlled wear as a design choice and excessive ripping as dated. This tracks with the brand's existing approach (distressing on most pieces but not shredded).

2. **Enzyme-washed and sulfur-dyed heavyweight fleece.** Brands are investing in custom enzyme washes and sulfur dyes on 450-500+ GSM French terry to recreate the hand-feel of a 30-year-old thrift find from day one. The finish produces subtle color shifts and soft fraying at cuffs without looking artificially aged. A step beyond standard acid wash — more muted, more tonal, and photographing better under natural light for social content.

3. **Layered vests as a third-piece silhouette.** Overshirts and vests are gaining traction as layering essentials, extending seasonal relevance and creating more dynamic outfit shots on TikTok and Instagram. The trend sits between utility-wear and casual streetwear — a distressed vest over a hoodie reads as layered without crossing into gorpcore territory.

4. **Neighborhood-exclusive and city-coded drops.** A distribution shift is underway where brands release hyper-limited colorways exclusive to a single city or neighborhood, building regional scarcity and community identity. This is more about drop strategy than product design, but it changes how pieces photograph and how communities engage with a brand on social.

5. **Zip hoodies as a Y2K-layering comeback.** Full-zip hoodies are re-emerging hard via Y2K nostalgia on TikTok, used as layering pieces over tees and under jackets. Washed or acid-washed zip-ups in particular are gaining traction because the open front reveals what's underneath — a natural fit for layering-and-reveal concepts.

### Design Directions

1. **Enzyme-washed zip hoodie in sky blue / faded navy.** Item: full-zip hoodie, 450+ GSM French terry. Finish: enzyme wash for tonal fading (not acid — softer, more lived-in). Colorway: sky blue body with faded navy at seams and hood, cursive logo embroidered on chest. Why it fits: uses the brand's signature color, plays into the zip-hoodie revival, and the enzyme wash is a step beyond the current acid-wash lineup without contradicting it.

2. **Distressed layering vest with hidden print lining.** Item: heavyweight cotton vest with raw-edge armholes. Finish: garment-dyed black with selective distressing at the hem and pocket edges. Colorway: washed black exterior, sky blue or muted leopard lining visible only when the vest hangs open. Why it fits: extends the brand's layering-and-reveal concept (like the snow leopard tracksuit) into a new silhouette. Vest sits over any hoodie in the current lineup.

3. **Sulfur-dyed matching set (hoodie + jogger) in stone/putty.** Item: pullover hoodie and tapered jogger set. Finish: sulfur dye on heavyweight cotton — each piece fades slightly differently, so the set looks intentionally imperfect rather than factory-uniform. Colorway: stone/putty base with charcoal cursive logo. Why it fits: neutral tonal palette sits next to the current navy and black pieces; the sulfur-dye technique is trending but not yet saturated in this price range ($60-$100 per piece is achievable at this weight and finish).

4. **Acid-washed cropped hoodie for warm-weather layering.** Item: boxy cropped pullover hoodie, hitting at the waist. Finish: acid wash in the brand's existing technique. Colorway: washed grey with sky-blue interior hood lining (visible when hood is down). Why it fits: a cropped version of the signature silhouette opens a warmer-weather category. The sky-blue hood lining is a subtle reveal detail. Boxy cropped hoodies are performing well on TikTok outfit content.

5. **Two-layer long sleeve tee — washed black over sky blue.** Item: long-sleeve fitted tee with a false double-layer construction (sky blue cuffs and hem peek out beneath a washed black outer layer). Finish: garment-washed black outer, unwashed sky blue inner trim. Colorway: black/sky blue. Why it fits: a lighter-weight entry point below the hoodie tier, uses the brand's layering language and signature color without repeating the tracksuit construction. Lands comfortably in the $60-$70 range.

### Competitor Spotlight

**Hellstar** is the most relevant competitor to watch right now. The LA-based brand has taken over the US distressed-streetwear lane in 2026 with oversized graphic hoodies and tees built on cracked prints, acid washes, and intentional distressing. Their February 2026 Hellstar x adidas x Anthony Edwards collab during NBA All-Star Weekend featured an all-over camo Superstar with ripped Three Stripes and flame accents alongside matching camo shorts, grey hoodie, and graphic tee — demonstrating how a distressed streetwear brand can cross into athletic collaborations without losing identity.

What's working: Hellstar's pieces look expensive and hand-damaged at the same time. Their limited-drop strategy creates genuine scarcity (stock doesn't come back), and the dark/gritty branding photographs extremely well for organic social content. The cracked-print and layered-distressing techniques are a step beyond standard acid wash.

Transferability: the aesthetic lane overlaps strongly — both brands live in distressed, oversized, washed territory. The difference is Hellstar leans gothic and graphic-heavy, while this brand's cursive-logo and tonal approach is softer and more wearable. The takeaway isn't to copy their darkness, but to study their finishing techniques (cracked print, layered distressing) and their drop discipline (true limited runs, no restocks). The athlete-collab model is also worth noting — a mid-tier streetwear brand partnering with a rising athlete can reach audiences that organic social alone can't.

### New Brands to Watch

1. **GRIFF // NYC** (@griffnycbrand) — Manhattan-based brand built entirely around acid-washed heavyweight hoodies and matching sweats. 420 GSM French terry, embroidered logos, no drawstrings. Each piece gets a unique acid-wash pattern. Small operation started in an apartment. Very close aesthetic match: acid wash, heavyweight, tonal, logo-driven. Worth watching for how they execute matching sets at accessible price points.

2. **Paly Hollywood** (@palyhollywood) — LA brand blending punk and grunge into distressed streetwear. Hand-worked pieces with raw fabric treatments, cracked graphics, and deliberate wear patterns. SS26 collection is inspired by infamous hotel stays and cultural figures. Higher price point (above this brand's range), but the finishing techniques — especially their hand-distressing and vintage-effect printing — are worth studying for treatment ideas that could be adapted at a lower tier.

3. **NONFLOOR** — Korean brand incorporating distressed denim and vintage-inspired sportswear with a rugged, contemporary look. Part of the growing Korean streetwear wave blending Western grunge references with Asian proportions and fabric innovation. Relevant because Korean brands are pushing wash and dye techniques (wax-wash, enzyme treatments) that haven't fully crossed over to Western independent streetwear yet.
