# Design Scan Log

## Scan 1 — 2026-09-13

### Trends

1. **Enzyme wash and sulfur dye replacing acid wash as the premium finish.** Brands are using biological enzyme washes and sulfur dyes to recreate the softness and tonal depth of a 30-year-old thrifted piece. The result is subtler and more natural than acid wash — less contrast, more character. This is showing up across TikTok hoodie reviews and emerging brand launches as the "next level" of vintage treatment.

2. **Garment dyeing as the new distressing.** Hoodies and sweats sewn in raw fabric and dyed as a finished garment are trending. The technique produces tonal variation at seams where thread absorbs dye differently, making each piece look one-of-one. It reads as elevated distressing without any physical destruction of the fabric.

3. **Fortnightly drops giving way to monthly or slower cadences.** EME Studios (€33M revenue) publicly shifted from fortnightly to monthly drops in 2026, citing the need for "slower communication" and more considered design. The market is rewarding quality and repeat wear over hype velocity — buyers are fatiguing on constant release pressure.

4. **Layered-volume construction in bottoms.** EME Studios' "I Miss Who I Was" collection features "dual" trousers with layered volumes — a construction technique that creates dimensional silhouettes through fabric doubling rather than simply widening the leg. This extends the "layering as design" concept from tops into lower-body pieces.

5. **Patchwork paneling as texture, not pattern.** Curved tonal paneling, applique embroidery, and studded detailing are gaining traction as surface treatments. The distinction from previous patchwork trends: the panels are tonal (same color family, different textures) rather than contrasting, keeping the effect textural rather than loud.

### Design Directions

1. **Enzyme-Washed Boxy Hoodie** — Boxy cropped hoodie in enzyme-washed sky blue heavyweight fleece. Light distressing at cuffs and hem. Tonal cursive logo (same blue, slightly darker shade). The enzyme wash is a natural evolution of the acid wash the brand already uses — softer gradients, more vintage feel — and sky blue in an enzyme wash creates a convincing sun-faded look that photographs well on social. $60–$80 range.

2. **Garment-Dyed Zip Jacket with Reveal Lining** — Zip-through jacket garment-dyed in charcoal. Seam bleeding reveals a lighter grey base. Interior lining in sky blue, visible at the collar, rolled cuffs, and side vents. This extends the brand's layered-reveal concept (snow leopard under acid wash) into outerwear territory. The garment-dye variation at seams makes each piece feel unique. $85–$100 range.

3. **Distressed Knit Shorts Set** — Matching heavyweight knit shorts and short-sleeve crew in heavy enzyme wash. Raw-edge hems, light pilling texture. Washed black with a navy undertone. Bridges the current acid-washed black tracksuit with the upcoming navy tracksuit in a warm-weather silhouette. Fills a gap in the lineup for summer drops. $60–$75 per piece.

4. **Slash-Reveal Long Sleeve Tee** — Oversized long-sleeve tee in acid-washed slate grey with strategic slash distressing across the chest and back. A sky blue mesh underlayer shows through the slashes. Translates the brand's double-layer reveal DNA into a lighter-weight piece that works as a layering mid-layer or standalone. Content-friendly: the grey-to-blue reveal pops on camera. $60–$70 range.

### Competitor Spotlight

**EME Studios** (Spain, founded 2017 by Conra Martínez and Gabriel Morón)

What they did: Closed 2025 at €33M revenue and shifted from fortnightly to monthly drops in 2026 to create "slower communication." Their January 2026 capsule "I Miss Who I Was" flips collegiate nostalgia through a distressed streetwear lens — hoodies with distressed finishes and embroidery, "dual" layered-volume trousers, multicolor zip bombers, studded jackets, leather caps and balaclavas. They're expanding into womenswear and opening international stores across the US, Germany, Netherlands, and UK.

What's working: The slower drop cadence lets each release build anticipation and hold value. The nostalgia angle (school uniforms reimagined as streetwear) gives every piece a story that drives social content. The distressed-plus-embroidery treatment on hoodies is close to this brand's lane.

Transferable? Partially. The slower cadence and story-driven drops are directly applicable — a comfy streetwear brand at $60–$100 doesn't need fortnightly chaos. The collegiate nostalgia angle is EME's own territory, but the method (take a familiar cultural reference, run it through distressed/washed treatments) is transferable. The embroidered-plus-distressed hoodie technique is worth studying as an evolution of the cursive logo placement.

### New Brands to Watch

1. **Paly Hollywood** (@palyhollywood, Los Angeles) — Founded 2020. Distressed graphics, hand-dye treatments, intentional raw fabric wear patterns. Every piece is crafted with deliberate imperfection — this is a brand that treats distressing as a design language, not a trend. The hand-worked, one-of-one energy aligns with the comfy streetwear lane. Higher-follower exception: the distressed/washed DNA is a direct aesthetic match.

2. **Racer Worldwide** (@racerworldwide, Tallinn, Estonia) — Founded 2017 by Mihhail Zigadlo at age 16. Distressed denim, slouchy silhouettes, washed treatments through an Eastern European lens. Now showing at Paris Fashion Week. The brand's use of distressed leather, faux fur layering, and ironic slogans on washed bases demonstrates how to keep the distressed aesthetic fresh with unexpected material combinations. Higher-follower exception: the distressed/layered approach closely mirrors this brand's design language.

3. **Taller de Rafa** (@tallerderafa, Bronx, NYC) — Founded during the pandemic by siblings Genesis and Rafael Valverde. Started as a screen-printing operation, now collaborating with Nike on the Air Max Goadome Low for FW2026. Their approach to community-first drops and quality construction is instructive — every piece ties back to Bronx auto-culture heritage through hardware details. Aesthetic is more industrial than washed, but the community-driven, quality-over-volume model is exactly the growth path for a brand at the $60–$100 level. Featured by Complex as a brand to know before it blows up.
