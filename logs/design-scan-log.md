# Design Scan Log

## 2026-09-23

### 1. Trends

1. **Sun-faded finishes overtaking aggressive distressing.** The biggest wash shift this season: sun-faded effects that mimic natural lightening from prolonged sun exposure are replacing heavier distressing as the default "worn-in" signal. TikTok is driving the technique into the mainstream with DIY tutorials, and brands are scaling it across denim, caps, hoodies, and cargo bottoms using potassium permanganate spray, laser, and enzyme-based methods. The effect reads softer and more intentional than acid wash or heavy sanding — gentle gradient fading concentrated on shoulders, hoods, pocket edges, and thigh panels. Who What Wear flagged sun-faded jeans as the must-have denim trend for spring 2026, and the aesthetic has carried straight into fall.

2. **Contrast stitching on heavyweight basics.** Visible contrast stitch is showing up across sweatpants, tees, and outerwear as a way to add texture and detail to muted, tonal palettes without adding graphics or branding. Particularly effective on washed or garment-dyed pieces where the stitch color pops against the softened base fabric. This is a low-cost design detail that photographs well and reads as intentional craft rather than decoration.

3. **"Full fit" capsule drops replacing single-item hype.** Brands like Greedy Unit are moving away from isolated hero-piece drops toward capsules of 7-9 coordinated pieces (tees, sweats, hats, accessories) that let buyers build a head-to-toe outfit from one drop. This drives higher AOV, produces better content (full-look styling photos vs. single-item flatlays), and reinforces brand identity. The hype-drop model hasn't disappeared, but the momentum is toward quality and completeness over scarcity.

4. **Balloon silhouettes in heavyweight fleece.** Wide, structured-but-soft silhouettes are gaining traction in sweatpants and fleece bottoms — 400+ GSM cotton, balloon fit, high-rise waist. The look is voluminous but not sloppy, photographing as a deliberate shape rather than an oversized afterthought. Shaka Wear's 3M Balloon Fleece Sweats are one example moving units, but the silhouette is showing up across independent labels too.

5. **Tonal texture layering replacing loud branding.** The dominant layering approach now is same-color-family, different-texture stacking — cotton tee base, terry or fleece mid-layer, nylon or waxed cotton outer. Three layers maximum, each adding a tactile contrast rather than a visual clash. The palette has moved firmly toward neutrals, deep browns, charcoal, olive, washed navy, and stone. Brands leading here are winning on feel-in-hand and photography rather than logo placement.

### 2. Design Directions

1. **Sun-faded heavyweight sweatshorts.** Item: mid-thigh sweatshorts in 350+ GSM French terry. Finish: sun-fade gradient on a sky blue base, concentrated at the hem and pocket edges, creating a natural-looking lightening effect. Colorway: sky blue fading to a paler ice blue at extremities. Cursive logo embroidered small at left thigh. Why it fits: fills a summer/transitional gap in the lineup with a product type the brand doesn't currently sell, applies the hottest wash trend of the season to the brand's signature color, and photographs well for social content. Estimated retail: $60-65.

2. **Layering vest with hidden print lining.** Item: washed shell vest with snap or button front. Finish: sun-faded black exterior shell (lightweight washed nylon or cotton canvas). Lining: tonal abstract wave print in charcoal and sky blue, visible when worn open or when the wearer flips the collar. Colorway: faded black exterior / charcoal-sky blue interior. Why it fits: directly extends the brand's signature layering-and-reveal concept into a new silhouette — vests are explicitly an approved direction and a product type not currently in the lineup. The hidden lining continues the "surprise underneath" DNA without repeating the snow leopard print. Estimated retail: $75-85.

3. **Two-layer heavyweight long sleeve with contrast cuffs.** Item: oversized long-sleeve tee in 300+ GSM cotton, constructed with a visible inner layer that extends past the outer at cuffs and hem. Finish: outer layer in acid-washed charcoal, inner layer in sky blue, creating a color-contrast reveal at the wrists and bottom edge without any cuts or slashes. Colorway: acid-washed charcoal / sky blue. Why it fits: the two-layer long sleeve is an explicitly approved direction that brings the brand's layering concept to a new format. The contrast-cuff detail photographs well in both flat-lay and on-body content and gives the piece an identity beyond a basic long sleeve. Estimated retail: $65-75.

4. **Distressed washed rugby shirt.** Item: heavyweight jersey rugby shirt with two-button placket. Finish: enzyme-washed with subtle distressing at the collar and cuffs, contrast stitching in sky blue along the placket and shoulder seams. Colorway: washed navy body with sky blue contrast stitch and white rubber buttons. Cursive logo embroidered at left chest. Why it fits: rugby shirts are a completely new silhouette for the brand, but the heavyweight jersey fabric, washed finish, and distressed detailing place it squarely in the existing aesthetic. The contrast stitch trend gives it a current edge, and the structured collar adds visual interest in content without reading preppy — the wash and distressing prevent that. Estimated retail: $70-80.

### 3. Competitor Spotlight

**Greedy Unit** (New York City)

What they did: Greedy Unit's June 2026 drop shipped 9 pieces simultaneously — tees, a crewneck, a hat, and accessories — designed as a coordinated capsule rather than isolated items. Standout piece was the Pin Helmut Tee: a long-sleeve with slightly distressed construction, purple-dyed cuffs and collar, and a boxy relaxed fit. Every piece in the drop shared a visual language (distressed finishes, dyed accent details, boxy cuts) that made them photograph as a collection rather than individual products.

What's working: the "full fit" drop strategy is producing stronger content cycles because buyers style the pieces together and post full-look photos rather than single-item shots. It also drives higher cart values — if the tee, the crewneck, and the hat all look like they belong together, buyers are incentivized to grab multiple items. Greedy Unit was voted Complex readers' best streetwear brand of 2025 and is sustaining momentum through 2026 by keeping drops intentional and limited.

Transferability: highly transferable. This brand already has the aesthetic range (distressed, washed, layered) to build capsule drops that span multiple product types. Instead of dropping a single tracksuit or a single hoodie, a capsule of 5-7 coordinated pieces (e.g., a washed long sleeve + sweatshorts + a cap + a vest, all in the same colorway family) would produce better social content and higher AOV. The key lesson is that every piece in the drop should share enough visual DNA to look like a set without being a literal matching set.

### 4. New Brands to Watch

1. **Paper Straight** (@paperstraight) — Amsterdam, Netherlands. An emerging brand blending street culture with craftsmanship, known for raw-edge detailing and distressed denim in relaxed baggy fits. Minimal branding, quality-first approach. Their distressed raw-edge finish reads as effortlessly worn-in without looking overworked. Worth watching for how they handle washed finishes on denim and basics at an accessible price point — their approach to controlled distressing without heavy graphics is close to this brand's lane.

2. **NONFLOOR** (@nonfloor) — Seoul, South Korea. Incorporates distressed denim and vintage-inspired sportswear with innovative washing techniques that produce a "sun-kissed" aesthetic. Known for striking cuts and patchwork details influenced by vintage workwear. The brand's approach to wash development — achieving faded, worn textures through process rather than graphics — is directly relevant. Their unisex positioning and emphasis on comfortable silhouettes overlap with this brand's DNA.

3. **Caracas Merch** (@caracasropa) — approximately 32K followers. A Venezuelan diaspora streetwear brand blending cultural identity with graphic pieces and accessories including bandanas. Less directly aligned on the distressed/washed aesthetic, but worth watching for their community-building approach: they've grown a loyal base at accessible prices by tying product drops to cultural storytelling. The model of identity-driven drops at the $60-100 range is relevant even where the product design language differs.
