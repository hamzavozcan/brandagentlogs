# Design Scan Log

---

## 2026-10-03

### 1. Trends

1. **Boxy-fit heavyweight construction is the default silhouette.** 400+ GSM French terry cotton, structured double hoods, and drop-shoulder cuts are now the baseline for premium streetwear tees and sweatshirts. Pre-shrunk, air-jet-spun yarn in heavyweight blends is what buyers expect. Brands not hitting this weight class read as fast fashion. This is relevant because it validates the brand's existing heavyweight approach but raises the bar on construction details like self-standing hoods and structured shoulders.

2. **Double-layer long sleeves are emerging as a standalone product format.** Mock-layered and true two-piece long sleeve tees — with contrast thermal, waffle-knit, or jersey sleeves — are showing up across wholesale platforms and DTC brands. MNML's double-layer thermal sleeve tee (acid-washed base, waffle-knit sleeve inserts) and Before the High Street's washed mock double-layer long sleeve (345 GSM heavyweight cotton) are reference points. The format is a natural fit for brands already working with layering as a design language.

3. **Quilted and puffer vests are filling the transitional layering gap.** Matte-finish puffer vests in black and muted tones — stand-collar and hooded — are dominating fall layering. The trend leans functional and minimal (not gorpcore utility), with clean surfaces and deliberate silhouettes. This is a relevant new silhouette for a brand built on layering, especially if the vest can carry the same reveal-lining concept.

4. **Controlled distressing is replacing random distressing.** The market is shifting from all-over distressing toward intentional, precisely placed treatments. Sun-fading simulation, hand-sanding for targeted wear patterns, and strategic enzyme washing are the techniques gaining ground. The key phrase from multiple sources: "authenticity beats perfection, but the distressing itself needs to look intentional, not accidental."

5. **Refined cargo pants with washed finishes are replacing extreme silhouettes.** The oversized, pocket-heavy cargo is losing ground to cleaner proportions with washed-black and olive finishes, contrast stitching, and ripstop textures. These are being styled as standalone bottoms (not part of matching sets), which opens a new product lane for brands that currently only sell upper-body pieces and full tracksuits.

### 2. Design Directions

1. **Distressed quilted vest with hidden print lining.** Item: lightweight quilted vest, stand collar, snap closure. Finish: heavy enzyme wash on a matte black shell, controlled distressing at the chest and hem. Lining: tonal sky-blue leopard print visible when unsnapped or layered open. Colorway: washed black shell / sky blue print lining. Fits the lineup because it introduces a new silhouette (vest) while carrying the brand's signature layering-reveal concept and sky blue color into a new format. Target: $75-$85.

2. **Two-layer long sleeve tee with contrast thermal sleeves.** Item: heavyweight cotton jersey body with waffle-knit thermal long sleeves in a contrasting wash. Finish: acid wash on the body, garment-dyed sleeves. Colorway: washed charcoal body / sky blue thermal sleeves. Fits because it applies the brand's two-layer DNA to a simpler, everyday format that doesn't exist in the current lineup. The contrast sleeve reads as intentional layering without needing to be an actual two-piece. Target: $60-$65.

3. **Washed cargo jogger with contrast stitch.** Item: tapered cargo jogger with two side-seam pockets and one thigh cargo pocket (minimal, not utility-overloaded). Finish: heavy garment wash in washed black, white contrast stitching on all seams. Fabric: 360 GSM French terry. Fits because the brand currently sells tracksuits but not standalone bottoms. A cargo jogger in the brand's wash language lets customers build outfits mixing pieces. Target: $70-$80.

4. **Heavyweight washed rugby shirt with cursive logo.** Item: long-sleeve rugby shirt with reinforced collar and rubber buttons. Finish: sun-fade wash for a lived-in look, distressed rib collar. Colorway: washed navy / sky blue collar stripe. Logo: cursive embroidery on left chest. Rugby shirts are gaining traction in streetwear right now and this is a new product type for the brand that sits naturally between a tee and a hoodie. Target: $65-$75.

### 3. Competitor Spotlight

**Cole Buxton — Leather Holdall Tote (August 2026)**

Cole Buxton, known for premium knit sportswear, released four versions of a leather Holdall Tote bag with finishes ranging from rich dark brown to distressed and textured variations. What's working: they extended their finish language (washed, textured, distressed) into a completely new product category (accessories/bags) without it feeling like a stretch. The distressed leather reads as the same brand that makes their washed knits. Transferability: the approach is directly relevant — the idea of taking a signature finish vocabulary and applying it to adjacent product categories (bags, headwear, scarves) rather than just more tops and bottoms. The risk is that accessories at this price tier ($60-$100) need to feel substantial, not like merch add-ons.

### 4. New Brands to Watch

1. **Racer Worldwide** (@racerworldwide) — Estonian brand started by a teenager, now producing heavyweight washed pieces with motorsport-inspired graphics. Their Washed Black Patch Hoodie (oversized fit, distressed inverted denim patches) and Washed Flag Hoodie (heavyweight washed zip with all-over distressed denim stripes) show a strong grasp of the distressed/layered aesthetic. Relevant because their wash treatments and heavyweight construction overlap with this brand's lane, but their graphic language (motorsport, flags) is distinct enough to not be a direct competitor. Watch for how they handle product diversification beyond hoodies.

2. **NONFLOOR** — Korean streetwear brand leaning into distressed denim and vintage-inspired sportswear. Part of the Korean wave alongside THISISNEVERTHAT and ADER ERROR, but positioned more rebelliously with rougher finishes and less-polished presentation. Worth watching because Korean streetwear brands are increasingly influencing Western independent labels on silhouette and proportion, and NONFLOOR's distressed approach is closer to this brand's lane than the cleaner Korean labels.

3. **Zic** (@zicstudios) — Founded by 21-year-old Isaac Allen. The standout product is a double-layered camo zip hoodie and perfectly faded jeans. The brand's focus on perfecting cut and shape on wardrobe staples (rather than graphics-heavy storytelling) is notable. Relevant because the double-layer construction concept directly overlaps with this brand's reveal/layering DNA, and they're attacking it from a basics-first angle that keeps the price point accessible.
