## 2026-09-16

### 1. Trends

1. **Funnel necks and structured collars on knitwear and fleece.** Runway-to-street crossover is accelerating in fall 2026. Funnel-neck sweaters and zip-through fleece pieces are appearing across TikTok OOTD content, replacing standard crewneck and hoodie silhouettes for top-of-fit pieces. The structure reads elevated without going techwear. Relevant because it opens a non-hoodie knitwear lane.

2. **Washed corduroy as a streetwear staple fabric.** Corduroy — particularly wide-wale and garment-dyed varieties — is moving into cargo pants, overshirts, and shorts across multiple streetwear labels. The fabric takes acid wash and enzyme wash treatments well and photographs with visible texture, which performs strongly in short-form video content. This is a material opportunity, not just a silhouette one.

3. **Monthly cadence replacing biweekly drops.** EME Studios publicly shifted from fortnightly drops to a monthly release schedule in 2026, citing "slower communication" and more considered designs. Other mid-tier European brands are following. The shift signals that the market is rewarding fewer, more polished releases over rapid churn — relevant for drop timing and content planning.

4. **Double-layer long sleeves gaining traction as a standalone category.** Mock double-layer long sleeves (a washed outer layer with a contrasting inner layer visible at the cuffs, collar, and hem) are appearing from manufacturers and indie brands as a distinct product type, not just an extension of hoodie layering. Before the High Street and INNBLAC are both stocking 345+ GSM heavyweight versions with vintage-dye finishes. This is a format the brand's layering DNA is built for but hasn't touched yet.

5. **Jorts and denim shorts as a hype vehicle for limited drops.** 99Based's SS26 drop centered jorts alongside polos and vests, using denim shorts as the anchor piece for summer content. The silhouette carries well in styled flat-lay and on-body social content. For a fall context, the equivalent play would be washed fleece shorts or heavyweight jersey shorts positioned as a transitional layering piece.

### 2. Design Directions

1. **Washed corduroy overshirt with hidden print lining — "The Cord Reveal"**
   Item: Oversized overshirt in wide-wale corduroy. Finish: enzyme-washed for a soft, faded hand-feel; sky-blue body with charcoal undertones from the wash. Lining: a tonal animal-print (muted leopard in grey-on-black) visible when cuffs are rolled and when the front is left open. Colorway: washed sky blue shell, grey-black interior print. Why it fits: takes the brand's reveal-layer concept into a new silhouette and fabric — an overshirt is not a zip jacket, and corduroy is a new material for the lineup. The wash treatment and cursive logo placement on the chest pocket keep it on-brand. Price-feasible at $80–$95.

2. **Double-layer long sleeve tee — "Ghost Layer"**
   Item: Heavyweight (350+ GSM) long sleeve tee with a shorter inner layer in a contrast color visible at hem, cuffs, and collar. Finish: garment-dyed and sun-faded wash on the outer layer. Colorway: outer layer in washed black, inner layer in sky blue — the signature color peeks through at all edges. Why it fits: this is the brand's layering DNA applied to a new product format. Not a hoodie, not a zip jacket. The double-layer construction photographs well for social (the color contrast at the cuffs is an instant content hook). Price-feasible at $60–$75.

3. **Funnel-neck fleece pullover — "Stack Neck"**
   Item: Heavyweight fleece pullover with an oversized funnel neck (can be worn up or folded). Finish: acid-washed in a two-tone fade, with cursive logo embroidered at the collar base visible when the funnel is folded down. Colorway: acid-washed charcoal-to-slate gradient. Why it fits: the funnel neck is a trending silhouette the brand doesn't sell, and the acid wash treatment is already in the brand's finishing vocabulary. The logo reveal when the collar folds is a subtle nod to the layering-and-reveal concept without being a zip jacket with a lining. Price-feasible at $75–$90.

4. **Washed fleece shorts — "Burnout Short"**
   Item: Heavyweight fleece shorts with a relaxed, above-the-knee fit. Finish: heavy enzyme wash for a sun-bleached, broken-in look. Distressed hems (raw-cut, not slashed). Cursive logo embroidered on the thigh. Colorway: washed navy (connecting to the upcoming navy tracksuit) or washed slate. Why it fits: the brand has no shorts in the lineup. Fleece shorts are a year-round content piece (styled with hoodies in fall/winter, solo in summer). The washed finish and distressed raw-cut hem stay in the brand's aesthetic lane without introducing a new concept — just a new product type. Price-feasible at $55–$65. Note: this is a transitional/summer piece; for an immediate fall release, position it as a layering bottom rather than a standalone.

### 3. Competitor Spotlight — EME Studios

**What they did:** EME Studios (Spain, founded 2017) shifted from fortnightly drops to a monthly cadence in 2026 while simultaneously expanding into womenswear and opening international retail in London, Amsterdam, and a New York pop-up. Their January 2026 "I Miss Who I Was" capsule leaned into nostalgic, prep-school-meets-streetwear styling — color-blocked oversized silhouettes with sporty stripes and uniform references, all within an oversized streetwear framework.

**What's working:** The slower release cadence is generating more focused social buzz per drop rather than diluting attention. The conceptual storytelling (nostalgia, school days) gives each collection a narrative hook that performs well in short-form video — influencers are building content around the theme, not just the product. The international retail expansion signals the brand is scaling beyond online-only DTC.

**Transferable or not:** The cadence lesson (fewer, more considered drops with narrative cohesion) is directly transferable. The prep/uniform aesthetic is not a match — it reads too clean and color-blocked for a distressed/washed brand. But the principle of building a short narrative around each drop (a mood, a reference, a place) rather than just shipping product is worth adopting. The womenswear expansion is also a signal that the European comfy-streetwear lane is big enough to support category growth.

### 4. New Brands to Watch

1. **Revered Armor** — @reveredarmor, ~18K followers. US-based. Tagline: "We forge symbolic armor for the modern collector." Elevated streetwear with sharp minimalism and premium detail. Their "Bullet Thermal" line and cropped baggy fits show willingness to experiment with silhouette. Muted palette (washed blacks, stone greys, forest greens). Not a direct aesthetic match — they're cleaner — but their approach to treating streetwear basics as collectible, limited pieces is a strategy reference worth tracking.

2. **Before the High Street** — @beforethehighstreet. UK-based. Specializes in heavyweight washed streetwear: 345–430 GSM pieces with vintage-dye and garment-wash finishes. Already stocking mock double-layer long sleeves and double-layer heavyweight hoodies. Their product development is extremely close to this brand's aesthetic territory — washed, heavyweight, layered — which makes them both a reference and a competitive signal. If they're already producing double-layer washed long sleeves, the market is moving.

3. **Demon Mentalityz** — @demonmentalityz. Miami, FL. Founded 2020, building momentum through limited drops and strong TikTok unboxing content. Offerings include zip-up jackets with custom all-over prints and flared sweatpants with adjustable bottom zippers. Their packaging game (frosted bags, branded accessories) is driving repeat content from buyers. Aesthetic leans slightly louder (full prints, flared cuts), but their drop mechanics and community-building approach on social are worth studying for a brand at a similar growth stage.
