## September 30, 2026

### 1. Trends

1. **Structured oversized replacing ultra-oversized.** The "wear it three sizes up" era is settling down. Silhouettes are still relaxed but more intentional — cleaner shoulder lines, straight-leg instead of ultra-wide, boxy but not ballooning. Multiple trend reports and brand releases confirm the shift toward proportions that look loose but are consciously styled. For distressed and washed pieces, this means slightly more shaped cuts that still read effortless.

2. **Rugby shirts and washed polo silhouettes entering streetwear.** Runway interest from Miu Miu, Prada, and Lacoste SS26 is filtering down into streetwear with oversized fits, vintage-wash finishes, and distressed collars. Brands are pairing them with distressed denim for a lived-in prep-meets-street look. This is a largely unclaimed silhouette in the distressed/washed lane — most brands doing rugby shirts are keeping them clean.

3. **Monthly drop cadences replacing biweekly.** EME Studios publicly shifted from fortnightly drops to monthly releases, framing it as "slower communication" for more considered designs. This reflects a broader pattern: small brands are pulling back from the two-week treadmill to put out fewer, higher-impact releases with more content per drop. Neighborhood-exclusive and city-specific micro-drops are also gaining traction as a scarcity lever.

4. **Vintage wash as a hero finish beyond denim.** Vintage-wash treatments — controlled fading, whiskering, enzyme washes, strategic distressing — are moving out of denim and into fleece, sweatpants, jersey, and knitwear. The "looks like it's been in rotation for years" finish is now a primary selling point across categories, not just a denim detail. Garment-dyed enzyme-wash tees and sweats are converting well on TikTok.

5. **Knit layering pieces as standalone streetwear.** Oversized knit vests and cardigans are being styled as primary layering pieces over tees and long sleeves. The texture contrast — chunky knit over washed cotton — is a core TikTok styling move that's driving purchase intent. Denim vests are also picking up for the same layering-without-bulk appeal. This opens a product category for brands rooted in layering.

### 2. Design Directions

1. **Acid-wash rugby long-sleeve.** Heavyweight rugby polo in acid-washed sky blue with the cursive logo small on the left chest. Distressed rib collar and cuffs. The rugby silhouette is trending (Trend #2) but nobody in the distressed/washed lane is claiming it yet — this puts the brand's signature finish on a silhouette with real momentum. Works as a layering piece under a vest or standalone. **Colorway:** acid-wash sky blue body, tonal navy collar stripe. **Price:** $70–$85.

2. **Washed knit vest with hidden print lining.** Oversized boxy knit vest in washed charcoal with sky blue ribbing at the V-neck and armholes. The inside lining uses the brand's existing snow leopard print so it flashes when the vest is pulled on or layered open. New silhouette that applies the brand's signature reveal/layering concept to knitwear, which is trending as a layering category (Trend #5). **Colorway:** washed charcoal body, sky blue trim, snow leopard interior. **Price:** $65–$80.

3. **Vintage-wash denim shorts.** Relaxed-fit, just-above-knee denim shorts in heavy vintage wash with subtle distressing at raw hems and pocket edges. Cursive logo embroidered small on the back pocket. A product category the brand doesn't currently sell, and vintage-wash denim shorts are a proven performer on TikTok for warm-weather content. **Colorway:** vintage-wash indigo. **Price:** $60–$75. **Timing note:** spring/summer item — design and sample now for an April–May drop window.

4. **Two-layer long sleeve with cuff reveal.** Heavyweight long-sleeve tee in washed black with extended ribbed cuffs in sky blue that peek out when layered or fold back to reveal a contrasting color block. Applies the brand's double-layer concept (currently only in the tracksuit) to a lighter, year-round piece. This is an approved direction that hasn't been explored yet. **Colorway:** washed black body, sky blue cuffs. **Price:** $60–$70.

5. **Acid-wash camp collar shirt.** Oversized short-sleeve camp collar shirt in acid-washed navy with tonal distressing and the cursive logo embroidered on the chest pocket. A warm-weather piece that fills a gap in the lineup — the brand has no shirts or woven pieces. The camp collar silhouette photographs well open over a long sleeve or tank for layered social content. **Colorway:** acid-wash navy, tonal distressing. **Price:** $65–$80. **Timing note:** spring/summer item — plan for the same drop window as the denim shorts.

### 3. Competitor Spotlight

**EME Studios** (Madrid, Spain)

EME Studios is making notable moves in 2026. The brand shifted from biweekly drops to a monthly release cadence, publicly framing it as investing in "slower communication" for more considered designs. Their "I MISS WHO I WAS" collection leaned into nostalgia-driven storytelling with layered-volume trousers, 12 polo iterations, distressed embroidered hoodies, and vibrant knitwear. They also launched womenswear ("EME for Everyone") in February and opened their first permanent store outside Spain on Carnaby Street in London, with a New York flagship in the pipeline. The brand reached approximately 33 million euros in revenue.

**What's working:** The monthly cadence gives each drop more breathing room for content, the nostalgia hook creates emotional connection that drives sharing, and the physical retail builds credibility for an online-first brand. Their collections consistently mix distressed treatments with knitwear and structured pieces, showing range without losing identity.

**Transferable?** The drop cadence shift is directly applicable — fewer, more considered releases with stronger content per drop. The nostalgia/storytelling approach (building a collection around a feeling rather than a season) translates well. The womenswear and retail expansion are relevant as longer-term markers but not immediately actionable at the current stage.

### 4. New Brands to Watch

1. **Amoses** (@amosesclothing) — Lille, France. An independent label building on textured materials, sober palettes, and cinematic limited drops. Each collection tells a different story through controlled washed finishes, voluminous knits, and reworked sherpas. The brand collaborated with European photographers and creatives to establish a visual style that mixes retro, mystery, and modernity. Growing quickly in the French independent scene. Relevant because: their textured-wash approach to knitwear and their limited-run storytelling model are close to this brand's lane.

2. **Midnight** (@about.midnight) — Germany. A one-person operation doing hand-painted vintage denim, founded by Can Demirezen. Every piece carries the handwriting of one maker. Gained enough visibility that MCM chose the brand for its first Global Creative Platform collaboration. Relevant because: the hand-finished, one-of-a-kind approach to vintage denim demonstrates how artisanal distressing techniques can elevate a small brand's perceived value without scaling production.

3. **Neutrale** — Spain. An environmentally-conscious label doing garment-dyed, washed-out soft goods with a soft, lived-in finish. Their enzyme-wash and garment-dye processes create tonal depth and texture that reads premium at accessible prices. Relevant because: the garment-dye wash process they use creates the kind of faded, lived-in look that fits the washed/distressed aesthetic, and their sustainability angle is increasingly important for the target demographic.
