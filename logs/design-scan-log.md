## 2026-09-24

### 1. Trends

1. **Multi-stage wash finishes are replacing single-process acid wash.** Brands are layering techniques — pigment dye base, sun-fade spray, enzyme wash — in sequence to produce surfaces that look organically worn rather than uniformly bleached. The manufacturing playbook for this is documented as a distinct shift: sun-faded and vintage-fade streetwear now requires a different production process than basic acid wash. This matters because the brand already does acid wash; stacking a sun-fade pass on top would create a finish that reads as more premium without changing the silhouette.

2. **Washed rugby shirts are crossing from prep into streetwear.** The rugby polo has become a dominant casual silhouette in 2026, styled oversized with wide-leg jeans and chunky sneakers. Miu Miu, Lacoste, and Prada all pushed the format on SS26 runways. The streetwear adoption specifically leans on garment-washed and distressed treatments to strip the preppy connotation — L.L.Bean's garment-washed rugby is selling on that exact premise. This is a silhouette the brand doesn't currently make.

3. **Knit sportswear is gaining ground as a fleece alternative.** Cole Buxton is leading this with knit track jackets and knit hoodies in alpaca-silk and Japanese double-faced knit cotton. The appeal is texture and drape over standard fleece construction. Knit tracksuits read as elevated without leaving the sportswear lane. This is more of a fabric/construction trend than a silhouette trend, but it changes what "tracksuit" can mean.

4. **Knit balaclavas have become a streetwear capsule-drop staple.** Corteiz, BAPE, and smaller brands are all running them as drop accessories. The format works because it's photogenic, cheap to produce, strongly brandable, and layers into outfit content naturally. The trend is still building momentum into fall 2026 rather than peaking.

5. **Heavyweight distressed cargo bottoms are a growth category.** Search interest in cargo pants nearly doubled between April 2025 and April 2026. The streetwear lane specifically favors washed, distressed, and paint-splashed treatments on relaxed-fit cargos — Carhartt WIP's distressed Double Knee shorts are a reference point. This is a bottom category the brand doesn't currently sell.

### 3. Competitor Spotlight

**Nude Project — "Airplane Mode" SS26 Campaign**

What they did: Closed out their Spring/Summer 2026 season with a campaign shot entirely without phones, inspired by Danny Boyle's "The Beach." The concept centers on disappearing from digital noise — the irony being that the campaign spread virally on the same platforms it critiques. The collection itself leans into linen and breathable fabrics (polos, casual tops), which is a deliberate move away from streetwear-only product.

What's working: The anti-digital angle generates organic conversation precisely because it's counterintuitive for a brand with 2.5M social followers. It also signals maturity — Nude Project is explicitly trying to "consolidate its offer and not depend solely on the streetwear wave." They now have 10 physical stores generating 45% of revenue and are expanding to LA, which means the brand-building is working beyond drops and hype cycles.

Transferable? The campaign concept is transferable — a smaller comfy streetwear brand could run a "no phones on set" shoot and let the behind-the-scenes footage carry the story on socials. The product direction (linen, breathable, minimal) is not transferable to a distressed/washed aesthetic. The broader lesson is that Nude Project is growing by building a world (travel, lifestyle, physical retail) rather than just releasing garments, and that playbook works at any scale.
