# Design Scan Log

---

## 2026-09-27

### 1. Trends

1. **Sun-faded and enzyme-washed finishes overtaking traditional acid wash.** Streetwear brands are shifting from aggressive acid wash toward subtler aging techniques — enzyme washes that soften fabric while fading color, and sun-fade treatments that create natural, uneven color transitions. The look is still lived-in but reads more refined. Multiple manufacturers are retooling production for these processes, and the finished results are showing up across TikTok lookbooks and Instagram drops. Matte blacks and enzyme-washed blacks are specifically having a moment.

2. **Double-layer short-over-long styling trending on TikTok.** The "Tonal-Tee Stack" — a long-sleeve tee layered under a short-sleeve in tonal colors like slate and charcoal — is circulating as a practical layering technique on TikTok. It creates depth without bulk and photographs well for social content. Emerging brands like Zic and Hidden Season are building this directly into two-layer product designs rather than leaving it as a styling hack.

3. **Cargo shorts with distressed washes in resurgence.** Search interest for cargo shorts nearly doubled between April 2025 and April 2026. The trending versions feature acid rinses, stone washes, and paint-splash details rather than clean utility finishes. Carhartt WIP's destroyed-wash bermudas with white paint micro-splashes are getting significant attention. The silhouette is long, loose, and paired with statement sneakers.

4. **Muted, material-first palettes dominating over loud graphics.** The 2026 color story across streetwear leans into washed blacks, clay browns, stone grays, and off-whites. Fewer loud graphics, fewer overloaded logos — the status signal is the fabric itself (its weight, wash, texture, and origin). This is a macro shift: well-cut basics in considered fabrics are reading more current than graphic-heavy pieces.

5. **Puffer and quilted vests becoming year-round layering staples.** Vests are being styled as mid-layers over hoodies and long sleeves across all seasons, not just as fall/winter outerwear. Matte black finishes and stand-collar models are trending. The silhouette fits naturally into layered streetwear outfits and keeps the torso structured without adding sleeve bulk.

### 2. Design Directions

1. **Two-layer long-sleeve tee — enzyme-washed black outer, sky blue inner.** A heavyweight long-sleeve where the inner layer in the brand's signature sky blue peeks through at the cuffs and hem of an enzyme-washed black outer. Draws on the double-layer styling trend (trend #2) and translates the brand's existing reveal/layering concept into a lighter-weight, year-round format that the lineup doesn't currently have. Estimated $65–$80.

2. **Distressed quilted vest with hidden tonal print lining.** A matte black quilted vest with a subtle dark-on-dark print lining (e.g., tonal paisley or abstract swirl — not snow leopard, which is already in the lineup) that reveals when unzipped or layered open. Rides the vest-as-layering-piece trend (trend #5) while applying the brand's reveal concept to a new silhouette. Estimated $80–$100.

3. **Sun-faded cargo shorts in sky blue-to-white gradient.** Heavyweight cargo shorts with a sun-fade treatment that transitions the brand's signature sky blue to near-white at the hems. Taps the cargo shorts resurgence (trend #3) and the sun-faded finish trend (trend #1). Extends the brand into bottoms — a category it doesn't currently sell — with a piece built for summer drops and social content. Estimated $65–$80.

4. **Enzyme-washed heavyweight pocket tee in stone gray.** A boxy, 300gsm+ pocket tee with an enzyme-washed stone gray finish and light distressing at the collar seam. The material-first trend (trend #4) makes this kind of blank-forward piece commercially viable right now, and it sits at the bottom of the price range (~$60) as an accessible entry point that still reads on-brand next to the hoodies and tracksuits.

### 3. Competitor Spotlight

**Corteiz** is the most notable mover right now. In 2026 alone: a Tokyo pop-up with the location kept secret until the morning of the drop, a Virgil Abloh Archives tribute capsule, a New Era headwear collab aligned to UK street culture, and a Nike Shox R419 confirmed for fall. All of this runs on their signature scarcity/mystery model — password-protected shop, one-day-only events, no traditional marketing spend. What's working: the community-first strategy creates demand that scales without diluting the brand identity. What doesn't transfer: Corteiz's aesthetic leans athletic and graphic-heavy, which doesn't match the distressed/washed lane. But the drop mechanics — secret-location pop-ups, mystery-driven engagement, region-exclusive capsules — are directly transferable and could amplify a washed/distressed product line that already photographs well for social content.

### 4. New Brands to Watch

1. **Zic (@zicmade)** — NYC-based, founded by 21-year-old Isaac Allen. Silhouette-driven brand that remixes familiar garments into new constructions (a nylon pullover hoodie that reads as a bomber jacket). Pop-ups in LA and Atlanta are drawing block-long lines. Not in the distressed/washed lane directly, but the garment-innovation approach — taking one known format and rebuilding it in a new material or construction — is a transferable playbook. Featured in Complex's "brands to know before they blow up in 2026."

2. **Organic Garmentz (@organicgarmentz, ~19K followers)** — Atlanta-based label blending Americana and military references into streetwear. Collaborated with Corteiz on their US pop-up tour. Leans more workwear-adjacent than this brand's lane, but worth monitoring for their community-building approach and how they've scaled through strategic collab partnerships while staying small-batch and independent.

3. **Daveed Baptiste (@daveedbaptiste)** — Haitian-born, NYC-based designer specializing in artisanal denim with sculptural surface treatments. His approach to fabric manipulation — warped denim patterns, heavy surface processing, controlled distressing — aligns with the brand's interest in wash and finish innovation. Recently did a Gap x Harlem's Fashion Row collab and is getting coverage from Complex and Business of Fashion. Smaller following, high signal for where surface-treatment craft is heading.
