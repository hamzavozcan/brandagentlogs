# Design Scan Log

## 2026-09-21

### 1. Trends

1. **Sun-faded and enzyme-wash finishes replacing standard acid wash as the premium signal.** Streetwear manufacturers are reporting a shift in specification requests toward "lived-in" finishes — sun-faded black, dusty indigo, chalk-washed olive, overdyed vintage brown — over the harsher acid-wash look. Pigment-dyed fleece in particular is gaining traction because it develops unique patina with wear, making each piece age differently. The technique reads as more premium and less aggressive than traditional acid wash while still landing in the distressed lane. Relevant to the brand's existing acid-wash DNA — this is an adjacent finish worth testing on future pieces.

2. **Washed rugby shirts and knit polos crossing into streetwear.** The rugby shirt has moved from prep revival into full streetwear rotation on TikTok and Instagram. The key shift in fall 2026 is toward washed, oversized rugby polos in muted tones (stone, washed blue, sage) as sweatshirt replacements. Brendon Babenzien's influence at J.Crew and the AW26/27 trend reports listing "knit polo" as a standout category confirm this isn't a micro-moment — it's a silhouette gaining real commercial weight. This opens a product lane the brand doesn't currently occupy.

3. **Layering vests as the core transitional piece.** Puffer vests and quilted vests are being styled as deliberate layering statements rather than functional outerwear. The trend emphasizes "warmth without bulk" and texture contrast — a vest over a hoodie or long-sleeve creates a proportional break that photographs well. Matte black and tonal earth finishes lead. The silhouette fits the brand's layering DNA and is explicitly a different product category from zip jackets.

4. **Heavyweight mesh and jersey shorts gaining year-round relevance.** Premium mesh shorts have evolved past basic athletic wear into a streetwear staple with better cuts, refined proportions, and elevated details. The styling formula — mesh short with an oversized washed tee or rugby top — is now a core TikTok outfit format. The upgrade from cheap athletic mesh to 300gsm+ cotton-blend jersey or premium polyester mesh is what makes them read as intentional rather than athleisure.

5. **Monthly cadence replacing fortnightly drops.** EME Studios publicly shifted to a monthly release schedule in 2026, citing "slower communication" and more considered design. The broader move is away from hype-cycle frequency toward fewer, more complete drops that give each piece breathing room on social. For a brand in the $60-$100 range, this pacing protects margins and avoids the "always on sale" perception that kills small brand equity.

### 2. Design Directions

1. **Enzyme-washed rugby polo — long sleeve, oversized, sky blue with stone contrast collar.** A heavyweight cotton rugby polo with the brand's cursive logo on the left chest, enzyme-washed for a soft, broken-in hand feel. Sky blue body with a stone or off-white contrast collar and placket. The enzyme wash gives it the lived-in look without the harshness of acid wash. Fits next to the current lineup because: it's a new silhouette (the brand has no collared tops), it uses the signature color, and the washed finish is on-brand. Should land at $65-$75.

2. **Pigment-dyed heavyweight sweatpants — wide leg, open hem, washed black.** 400gsm+ french terry, pigment-dyed in washed black so the color has natural variation and will fade further with wear. Wide leg, open hem that stacks over sneakers. Cursive logo small on the left hip. The brand currently sells tracksuits (matched sets), but standalone heavyweight sweatpants in a pigment-dye finish are a different product — sold as individual bottoms, not part of a set, with a premium wash that distinguishes them from the tracksuit pants. Should land at $70-$80.

3. **Quilted vest with hidden print lining — matte black shell, snow leopard or tonal sky blue interior.** A lightweight quilted vest with a matte black outer and the brand's snow leopard print (or a tonal sky blue graphic) on the interior lining, visible when unzipped. The vest is a new silhouette that fits the layering-and-reveal concept without being another zip jacket — it's sleeveless, changes the proportions, and layers over hoodies and long-sleeves from the existing lineup. Should land at $75-$85.

4. **Sun-faded long-sleeve tee — two-layer construction, contrast cuff reveal.** A heavyweight long-sleeve tee with a slightly extended body, treated with a sun-fade finish that lightens the fabric unevenly from shoulder to hem. The inner layer extends past the outer at the cuffs and hem in a contrasting tone (e.g., sun-faded black outer with a sky blue inner cuff peeking out). This applies the brand's two-layer concept to a new format — a long-sleeve tee rather than a zip jacket or tracksuit top — and the sun-fade finish is a step beyond the existing acid wash. Should land at $60-$70.

### 3. Competitor Spotlight

**EME Studios — scaling from Spanish streetwear label to international retail brand.**

EME Studios (founded 2017, Spain) made a notable move in 2026: opening at 57 Carnaby Street in London (their first permanent store outside Spain), launching their first womenswear collection ("EME for Everyone"), and planning further openings in Amsterdam and a New York pop-up. They also publicly shifted from fortnightly drops to a monthly cadence.

What's working: EME built a clear design identity (minimalist European streetwear with considered proportions) and scaled it without diluting — the womenswear launch draws from Japanese tailoring rather than just shrinking the menswear. The Carnaby Street location signals serious intent without the overhead of a flagship.

Transferable? Partially. The international retail expansion isn't relevant at this brand's stage, but the cadence shift and the approach to womenswear (entering a new category with a distinct design point rather than just re-gendering existing pieces) is a useful framework. The monthly drop model in particular is worth studying — it's a signal that the market is rewarding patience over frequency at this tier.

### 4. New Brands to Watch

1. **Unfounded Studios** (@unfoundedstudios) — UK-based streetwear brand, ~16K Instagram followers, ~30K on TikTok. Runs limited drops with hoodies, joggers, tees, and shorts. The aesthetic is urban and edgy with an emphasis on quality materials. They're building community through scarcity and direct TikTok engagement rather than influencer seeding. Worth watching for how they handle the transition from drop-driven hype to a repeatable product line — a challenge every brand in this lane faces at around this size.

2. **Daveed Baptiste** (@daveedbaptiste) — New York-based, Haitian-American designer specializing in artisanal distressed denim. Uses laser-etching, hand-distressing, and sculptural construction on denim pieces. Recently collaborated with Gap x Harlem's Fashion Row (Feb 2026) and held a solo exhibition through NYC's Materials for the Arts. His approach to denim surface treatment is genuinely novel — not just ripped jeans but architecturally distressed pieces. Relevant because his finishing techniques (laser-etching, controlled distressing) could inform how the brand thinks about distressing beyond random tears.

3. **Racer Worldwide** (@racerworldwide) — Founded by teenagers in Tallinn, Estonia in 2017, now one of the more interesting European labels. Hybrid drop/seasonal model. The aesthetic sits at the intersection of steampunk, goth, and post-Soviet youth culture with distressed denim, slouchy silhouettes, and dark palettes. They're proof that Eastern European streetwear has its own lane outside the London/Madrid/Paris axis. Worth watching for their denim treatment and willingness to mix subculture references without landing in costume territory.
